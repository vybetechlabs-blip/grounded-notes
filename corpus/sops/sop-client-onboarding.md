# SOP: Client Onboarding

Owner: Noor Haddad. Runs from contract signature to kickoff call. Target: kickoff
within 7 business days of signature.

## Steps

1. **Countersign and file the contract** (day 0). Noor files the executed copy
   and creates the engagement folder from the template.
2. **Send the welcome packet** (day 0–1). Includes the team intro sheet, the
   kickoff agenda, the access-request list, and the invoice for the first 50%
   (see Engagement Models and Pricing for payment terms).
3. **Request access** (day 1). Send the access-request list generated during
   discovery. Access is requested read-only first; write access only when the
   build needs it. Every credential goes into the client's secret manager per
   the Data Handling and Privacy SOP.
4. **Create the project board** (day 1). One kanban board per engagement,
   pre-loaded with the milestone template for the service type.
5. **Schedule the standing calls** (day 2). Weekly 30-minute status call, fixed
   day/time for the whole engagement (see Weekly Status Reporting SOP).
6. **Internal staffing huddle** (day 2–3). Delivery lead walks the team through
   the discovery notes and flags risks. Fifteen minutes, no slides.
7. **Confirm access received** (day 3–5). The engagement cannot proceed to
   kickoff until every item on the access list is confirmed working. Chasing
   access is Noor's job, not the engineer's.
8. **Kickoff call** (day ≤7). Run per the Project Kickoff SOP.

## Blockers and escalation

If access is not complete by day 5, Noor escalates to Theo, who calls the
client sponsor directly. If the kickoff invoice is unpaid by day 21 after
kickoff, work pauses automatically — the delivery lead does not make this
call; it is contractual.

## Common mistakes

- Kicking off without confirmed access "to keep momentum." Never. Every time
  we have done this the first sprint week was wasted on access chasing.
- Letting the client skip the welcome packet questionnaire. It feeds the
  kickoff agenda; without it the kickoff call runs long and vague.
